Choose and publish RahRow own-code licensing with qualified legal review. Use Apache-2.0 as the leading candidate because it permits personal use, personal builds, internal company use, public forks and maintenance, redistribution, and commercial use while providing patent terms and preserving license, copyright, trademark, and attribution notices. Compare MPL-2.0 if file-level copyleft and publication of distributed modifications better matches the maintenance goal; document why MIT or stronger copyleft alternatives were accepted or rejected.

RahRow must not claim OSI open-source status while restricting commercial VPN, proxy, utility, or competing-product use: the Open Source Definition requires free redistribution and no discrimination by field of endeavor. Express the credit goal as a universal redistribution obligation through the selected standard license and an accurate RahRow attribution notice, not a competitor-only restriction. Add a separate trademark and representation policy so forks cannot imply official RahRow sponsorship without restricting code use.

Publish plain-language licensing guidance covering personal use, internal company use, personal builds, public maintenance, modified distributions, commercial products, source obligations, required RahRow credit placement, trademarks, contribution licensing, dependency boundaries, and contact/escalation for ambiguity. Align repository metadata, package manifests, contributor docs, website/About copy, source headers or SPDX identifiers, binary artifacts, and stores only after counsel approves the exact model.
